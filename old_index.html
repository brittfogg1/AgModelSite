1. The problem we are solving

For 15 years, Crystal Ball has been the system our teams relied on to manage the full chicken production cycle — from breeder selection through egg hatching, pullet development, and broiler yield. The name said everything: you needed a crystal ball to predict how many chicken breeds to select, how much feed to order, how long birds would occupy each house, and what yield each flock would produce in a given year.

Crystal Ball served us well. But it was built for a world where the integrator, the breeder farm, and the hatchery each worked in relative isolation. Today all three parties need to operate from a single shared system of truth — with actuals versus forecast visible at every stage of the production cycle.

The replacement platform must do what Crystal Ball could not:

  • Connect breeders, hatcheries, and integrators in one shared operating system
  • Track actuals versus forecast from breeder selection through final broiler yield
  • Give every user — regardless of their role — a clear view of flock performance at any point in the cycle
  • Scale to support new animal proteins and production types as the business grows

2. The naming journey

The search for the right name covered significant ground. Below is a summary of the key directions explored and why each was ultimately set aside or advanced.

What we ruled out

Direction Examples Why set aside
Generic SaaS BreederOS, FlockOS, HatchOS “OS” is overused across every industry. Blends in rather than stands out. Also undersells the full scope of the platform.
Single-stage names BreederOS, HatchTrack Leading with one stage (breeder, hatch) alienates the other two user groups before they log in.
Poultry-generic names FlockLedger, NestYield, YieldLink Strong names but too narrow for a platform that may expand beyond poultry over time.
ClearView + agricultural word ClearView FlockCycle, ClearView HarvestCast, ClearView BroodScope Shortlisted and documented for stakeholder feedback. Strong poultry names but still product-level, not platform-level thinking.

The breakthrough: platform thinking

The naming conversation shifted when we stopped thinking about what the product tracks and started thinking about what the integrator fears most: invisible loss. A flock underperforms and no one knows if it was the bloodline, the breeder’s management, the hatchery’s hatch rate, or the grow-out conditions. The loss has already happened by the time anyone sees it.

The right platform name should not describe a feature. It should describe a methodology — the way a modern poultry operation thinks about production intelligence. That reframe led directly to AgModel.

3. The recommended name: AgModel

“You used to need a crystal ball. Now you have AgModel.”

AgModel works at the platform level in a way no other candidate does. Here is why:

Reason What it means in practice
Owns the category AgModel does not describe a feature — it describes a methodology. You are not just tracking chickens, you are modeling agricultural production. That is a bigger, more defensible idea.
Scales infinitely AgModel works for poultry today. It works for hogs, cattle, or row crops tomorrow. The name does not lock the platform into a single commodity.
“Model” is the right word Actuals versus forecast is modeling. Breeder selection to yield prediction is modeling. The name earns its meaning from the work the platform actually does.
The product family writes itself AgModel Breeder. AgModel Hatch. AgModel Broiler. Each module is immediately understood by its intended user without explanation.
Lives inside our company AgModel does not need to carry company credibility on its own. Our company provides the trust. AgModel is the product identity that our three user groups rally around.

4. The Crystal Ball legacy bridge

After 15 years, Crystal Ball is not just software — it is shared language. People say “What does the Crystal Ball say?” and “Run it through Crystal Ball.” Replacing that language requires a transition story that honors the past while declaring something better has arrived.

From Crystal Ball

To AgModel

From prediction to precision. From guessing to modeling.

Crystal Ball implied mystery — you were peering into an uncertain future hoping for the right answer. AgModel removes that uncertainty. It does not guess what might happen. It models what is happening and why — and tells every party in the chain what to expect before the cycle ends.

6. Name availability

A search of the commercial software landscape confirms that AgModel is not in use as a product or platform name in agriculture or poultry.

Finding Detail
Commercial use No company is using AgModel as a commercial software product or brand in the agriculture industry. The name is open in the marketplace.
Academic project An open-source academic simulation model shares the name AgModel. It is a university research project with no commercial presence, no trademark, and no overlap with poultry production software.
Closest competitor MTech Systems markets a platform called Amino, engineered for poultry producers and covering breeder performance through grow-out and processing. This is the most comparable product in the market and worth monitoring.
Recommended next step Commission a formal USPTO trademark search before committing to the name. Initial landscape is clean.
